## Positioning & Messaging

**Primary value proposition**: **The UK's authoritative source for current savings and mortgage rates.** Used as data source by major news outlets.

**Target audience**: Active savers and remortgagers comparing rates; financial journalists; institutional users.

**Positioning angle**: **The data-source comparison engine.** Different from RichQuid — they sell live tables, RichQuid offers ranges + steers users to live aggregators (this very site).

**Key messaging themes**:
- Whole-of-market comparison
- Continuously updated rate data
- Long-running industry reputation
- Best-buy lists for cash ISAs, savings accounts, mortgages, credit cards, personal loans

## Product & Features

- **Live rate tables** for: cash ISAs, savings accounts, fixed-rate bonds, regular savers, current accounts, credit cards, personal loans, mortgages
- **Best-buy lists** updated daily
- **Comparison filters** by deposit, term, type
- **Email rate alerts**
- **Mortgage broker introducer** service (commercial)
- **B2B data products** for financial institutions
- **Moneyfacts Group plc** also operates trade/intermediary services (a B2B side most readers don't see)

## Monetisation

Comparison advertising (lender placement) + B2B data licensing. Affiliate commercials from rate clicks. Editorial independence is claimed via standard disclosures.

## SEO & Content Strategy

- **Very high DR** for rate-related queries — incumbents for "best savings rates UK", "best cash ISA"
- **Continuously refreshed product database** means rate tables stay fresh and rank
- **Some editorial guide content** alongside the rate tables — moderate depth
- **Brand search volume**: high — many people search "Moneyfacts" directly

## Strengths

- **The benchmark for UK rate data** — even MSE and the BBC cite Moneyfacts figures
- **Live, daily-refreshed tables** that ordinary editorial sites can't match
- **Long brand history** (1988) lends authority
- **B2B + B2C model** diversifies revenue (resilient against ad downturns)

## Weaknesses

- **Editorial/explanatory content is thinner** than MSE/Which?/NimbleFins
- **UI dated** in places (the rebrand is partial)
- **Brand recognition** is high among savers but lower among general public than MSE
- **Comparison page UX** can feel cluttered with lender labels and sponsored slots
- **Less narrative-driven content** — readers wanting "should I fix for 2 or 5 years" find better answers elsewhere

## Competitive implications for RichQuid

**Where they're strong vs RichQuid**: Live rate data (RichQuid deliberately doesn't compete here — see /best-savings-rates page which steers users to Moneyfacts). Brand authority on rates specifically.

**Where RichQuid can differentiate**:
- **Explanatory editorial depth** around rates — "what to do with the rate" vs "what is the rate"
- **Currency of tax-and-rule changes**: RichQuid covers April 2026 dividend rises, April 2027 IHT changes; Moneyfacts focuses purely on rates
- **Calculator depth**: RichQuid's 9 calculators outclass Moneyfacts' simpler ones
- **Niches**: any topic beyond rate aggregation — visa-holder mortgages, expat ISAs, IHT planning

**Strategic note**: Moneyfacts is **less a competitor than a complement**. RichQuid's /best-savings-rates page already correctly defers to them. The relationship can stay that way — link to Moneyfacts for live rate data, win the explanatory traffic ourselves.
