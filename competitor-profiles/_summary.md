## Landscape overview

UK personal finance is a **mature, well-served, but heterogeneous category**. No single competitor combines all of: free + open access, depth of editorial, calculator interactivity, plain-English tone, and currency on regulatory changes. Each major player owns one or two of these strengths and trades off the others. The market is dominated by **MoneySavingExpert** in mass-market traffic, **MoneyHelper** in trust signalling, and **Which?** in editorial authority — with several specialist players (Moneyfacts for rate data, MoneyWeek for investment, This is Money for news, NimbleFins for comparison research) carving narrower territory.

## 5 strategic observations

### 1. Nobody owns the "free + depth + current + interactive" quadrant credibly yet

MoneySavingExpert is free + current but light on long-form depth (it's news-format). Which? is deep + authoritative but paywalled. MoneyWeek is deep but investment-skewed and paywalled. NimbleFins is closest in shape but affiliate-driven and shallower on tax/IHT/pensions. **This quadrant is RichQuid's natural home** — and it's where the market is genuinely under-served.

### 2. Tax/IHT/regulatory currency is broadly under-covered

Every competitor mentions the April 2026 dividend rate rise and April 2027 changes — but mostly as news ticks rather than systematic explainer guides. RichQuid's recent batch of articles on:

- Dividend tax rate rise April 2026
- Cash ISA cap £12,000 from April 2027
- Pensions and IHT from April 2027
- Making Tax Digital ITSA April 2026
- State Pension Age 66→67 phase-in

...is materially better-organised than any competitor's equivalent coverage today.

### 3. Visa-holder / expat / new-arrival finance is genuinely vacant

None of the seven profiled competitors covers these topics with the depth RichQuid does. MSE has one or two old visa-mortgage guides; Which? has limited expat material; MoneyHelper is silent on visa-specific finance. This is a defensible niche RichQuid already owns.

### 4. Calculator quality is uniformly mediocre across the field

MSE's Cheap Energy Club is the only competitor calculator that genuinely shines (because it queries live energy quotes). The rest are black-box tools that produce a number without showing the working. RichQuid's "shows the maths" approach is a real, defensible differentiator.

### 5. E-E-A-T is the most fragile competitive position

For YMYL content (personal finance qualifies), Google increasingly weights author identity, credentials and editorial trust signals. The competitor landscape on this:

- **Which? Money**: strong (named experts, 60+ year brand)
- **MoneyHelper**: strongest (government-backed)
- **MoneySavingExpert**: strong (Martin Lewis personal brand)
- **MoneyWeek**: strong (named journalists, magazine credibility)
- **This is Money**: moderate (named columnists, but tabloid association)
- **Moneyfacts**: moderate (industry data brand, but no editorial face)
- **NimbleFins**: weak (no public-facing author identity)
- **RichQuid**: weak — currently a pseudonymous "Clara Penny" without bio or credentials

**This is RichQuid's biggest single competitive vulnerability.** The SEO audit flagged it as Critical. It needs resolving.

## Gaps and opportunities

### Where the market is under-served (RichQuid can exploit)

1. **Visa-holder finance** — mortgages, pensions, ISAs for Skilled Worker / ILR / Health and Care visa holders. Already a RichQuid strength; lean into it.
2. **Post-2025 non-dom regime explainer content** — the FIG (Foreign Income and Gains) regime is barely covered by competitors. RichQuid has one article; could be a sub-pillar.
3. **Director-shareholder limited company finance** — IR35, dividend extraction, employer pension contributions. NimbleFins has scattered content; nobody owns it.
4. **"What changed this tax year" annual evergreen** — competitors do single news pieces but no comprehensive year-on-year comparison. RichQuid's existing what-changed-this-tax-year guide is a structural moat if updated annually.
5. **Calculator + interpretation hybrids** — every competitor has either calculators OR articles; few have calculators that connect directly to in-depth interpretation articles.
6. **Plain-English current-affairs explanation** — when a budget happens, MSE and This is Money rush news; Which? takes weeks; MoneyHelper takes months; RichQuid can be the calm 48-hour explainer.

### Where competitors are immovable (don't compete)

1. **Live rate tables** — Moneyfacts owns this. RichQuid's /best-savings-rates page correctly defers.
2. **Mass-market deals** — MSE has 16M weekly email subscribers. Don't try.
3. **Pension Wise guidance** — only MoneyHelper can deliver this; link to it.
4. **Cheap Energy Club** — MSE only.
5. **Consumer testing** of insurance/products — Which? owns this.
6. **Daily financial news** — This is Money + MoneyWeek can publish 10x volume.

## Recommended strategic emphasis for RichQuid

Based on the landscape:

1. **Lead with editorial depth and current-year accuracy** — the under-served quadrant.
2. **Own the visa / expat / new-arrival niche** unapologetically. Pillar page + ~20 sub-articles.
3. **Resolve E-E-A-T weakness urgently** — named author with credentials, or fully-disclosed editorial persona (Clara Penny as openly an editorial brand persona, like The Economist's anonymity model).
4. **Don't chase MSE's volume or speed** — chase Which?'s depth and MoneyWeek's quality.
5. **Compete with Moneyfacts via interpretation, not via rate data** — link to them, win the "what to do with this rate" traffic.
6. **Build content clusters around topics where you uniquely combine calc + guide + recent-change article**: e.g. dividend tax (calc + guide + April 2026 change), ISA wrapper (calc + types + April 2027 changes), pension IHT (calc + guide + April 2027 change).
