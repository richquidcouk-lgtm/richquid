## Positioning & Messaging

**Primary value proposition**: **Free, impartial, government-backed guidance** on money and pensions. The "impartial" framing is the killer differentiator — no other major UK finance site can credibly claim it.

**Target audience**: Broad UK population, with explicit focus on people at life-event transitions — buying/renting a home, separation, divorce, job loss, bereavement, having a baby, retirement.

**Positioning angle**: **The official safety net.** Where you go when you don't trust commercial sites.

**Key messaging themes**:
- "Free, impartial, backed by government"
- Help in plain English for difficult life moments
- Both online guidance and phone/face-to-face support
- Pension Wise (the highest-trust UK pension guidance service for over-50s) lives under this brand

## Product & Features

- **Guides** across budgeting, debt, savings, mortgages, insurance, pensions, retirement, divorce, bereavement, work, benefits
- **Calculators**: budget planner, pension calculator, debt management
- **Phone helpline** (multiple specialist lines)
- **Face-to-face Pension Wise appointments** (free for over-50s)
- **Webchat**
- Available in Welsh

## Monetisation

**None — completely free, no ads, no affiliates.** This is structural; the funding comes from regulated levies.

## SEO & Content Strategy

- Inherits high DR from gov.uk-adjacent positioning; ranks well for "MoneyHelper [topic]" + many generic "help with [topic] UK" queries
- Content is **highly scannable, plain-English, well-structured** — the design uses the same govt-style accessible layout pattern
- Content cadence: lower than commercial sites, but updates align with regulatory changes
- Often cited by other finance sites (RichQuid included) for authoritative figures

## Strengths

- **Unmatched trust positioning** — government-backed, impartial
- **No commercial conflict of interest**
- Specialist services (Pension Wise) that no commercial competitor can replicate
- Comprehensive life-event coverage
- Multi-channel (phone, in-person, web)
- Accessible language and design

## Weaknesses

- **Conservative content**: rarely takes a strong editorial position (by design — they can't recommend products)
- **No real-time deal/rate intelligence**: doesn't tell you which provider is best
- **Less actionable**: ends at "speak to an adviser" rather than "do this now"
- UI is functional, not particularly engaging
- Brand recognition lower than MoneySavingExpert despite being officially-endorsed

## Competitive implications for RichQuid

**Where they're strong vs RichQuid**: Trust (government-backed), specialist services (Pension Wise), zero-conflict guidance reputation.

**Where RichQuid can differentiate**:
- **Editorial voice and depth**: MoneyHelper has to stay neutral; RichQuid can take a clear position
- **Calculators that show working**: MoneyHelper's tools are good but black-boxed; RichQuid's show the maths
- **Topical / news content**: MoneyHelper rarely covers breaking changes; RichQuid can cover the dividend rise, IHT-on-pensions changes etc. in current context
- **Niches**: visa-holder finance, expat flows, non-dom transition

**Strategic note**: MoneyHelper is **not really a competitor in commercial terms** — it's a complement. Link to it heavily on RichQuid (already done in many guides). Borrowing its trust by association is more valuable than competing with it.
