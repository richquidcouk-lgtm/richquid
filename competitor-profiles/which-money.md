## Positioning & Messaging

**Primary value proposition**: **Independent consumer testing and expert advice on money** — the same brand reliability that tests washing machines applied to financial products.

**Target audience**: Mid-to-affluent UK households who value impartial expert recommendations and are willing to pay a subscription for "best buy" guidance.

**Positioning angle**: **The independent consumer authority.** Distinct from MSE (which is more news/deals-driven) and from gov-backed MoneyHelper (which can't recommend).

**Key messaging themes**:
- Expert testing, reviews and advice
- Make your money go further
- Avoid scams and grow your savings
- Named experts quoted in editorial (Grace Witherden, Gabriel Nussbaum)

## Monetisation

Subscription-led — distinct from MSE's affiliate model. Memberships fund the editorial operation. No display ads.

## SEO & Content Strategy

- **High DR**: as a 60+ year old consumer brand with .co.uk + gov reference frequency
- **Premium content gated**: the most valuable "Best Buys" guidance sits behind the paywall
- **News + guides hybrid**: free news drives traffic, premium guides convert
- **Editorial pattern**: clear expert bylines and named credentials throughout

## Strengths

- **Strongest "expert testing" reputation in UK consumer media** — moat built over 60+ years
- **Subscription model removes affiliate conflict** — readers know best buys reflect testing, not commission
- **Named, credentialed experts** = strong E-E-A-T for YMYL
- Phone helpline for members is genuinely useful (rare in finance media)
- Cross-domain trust (people who trust Which? Reviews trust Which? Money)

## Weaknesses

- **Paywall reduces top-of-funnel SEO** — best content not indexed by Google
- Subscription cost barrier — won't capture price-sensitive readers MSE serves for free
- Some "best buy" lists update less frequently than competitor rate trackers
- UI is less polished than other premium consumer brands
- Brand skews older — younger audiences may not know Which? deeply

## Competitive implications for RichQuid

**Where they're strong vs RichQuid**: Brand authority (60+ years), named-expert E-E-A-T, paywall-funded editorial independence.

**Where RichQuid can differentiate**:
- **Free + open access**: RichQuid's content is fully open, no paywall — better for SEO and reach
- **Interactive calculators with editable inputs**: Which? has tools but tends to gate them
- **Calmer editorial design** + cleaner reading experience
- **Tax-year currency**: RichQuid's content is more dynamic on recent budget changes (Apr 2026 dividend rise, Apr 2027 IHT-on-pensions) — Which? sometimes lags on these
- **Coverage of niches Which? skips** — visa-holder finance, expat money, crypto declaration

**Threats**: Which? Money is the closest analog to RichQuid in editorial voice (sober, authoritative, plain-English). If RichQuid scales, Which? could pressure on E-E-A-T more easily than MSE could.
