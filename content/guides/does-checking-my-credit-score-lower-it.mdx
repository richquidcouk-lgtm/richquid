**No. Checking your own credit report or credit score is always a soft search, and soft searches never affect your score — no matter how many times you check, or which app or credit reference agency you use.** The confusion usually comes from mixing this up with hard searches, which happen when you actually apply for credit and can have a small, temporary effect.

## Soft search vs hard search — the actual difference

- **Soft search**: a check that doesn't leave a mark visible to other lenders. Covers checking your own score, eligibility checkers, and background checks some employers or landlords run with your permission. Has no effect on your score.
- **Hard search**: a full credit check, recorded on your file and visible to other lenders, that happens when you **formally apply** for credit — a credit card, loan, mortgage or, in some cases, a mobile contract.

[Experian's own guidance is explicit](https://www.experian.co.uk/consumer/guides/searches-and-credit-checks.html): "checking your own credit report or credit score won't affect your score or your likelihood of being accepted for credit — no matter how many times you check them." Eligibility checkers (the "check your chances before you apply" tools most card and loan comparison sites offer) also use soft searches — you only get a hard search once you go ahead and submit a full application.

## Why this catches people out

The word "check" does double duty, and that's the whole confusion: checking your own file (soft, no effect) and a lender checking your file when you apply (hard, small effect) sound like the same action but aren't. A few practical implications:

- You can check your credit score as often as you like — weekly, even daily — with zero cost to your score.
- Using an eligibility checker before applying for a card is free information: it estimates your approval odds using a soft search, so you can rule out weak matches before triggering a hard search.
- The only thing that actually affects your score here is submitting the **full application**, not the checking that comes before it.

## What hard searches actually do

A single hard search typically has a small, temporary effect on your score. The risk isn't one search — it's several in a short window, which can look to a lender like you're suddenly seeking a lot of credit at once. Hard searches stay visible on your UK credit file for **12 months**, though their effect on your score fades well before then.

If you're planning a big application — a mortgage, for example — it's worth avoiding unrelated credit applications (a new card, a phone contract, a car finance deal) in the months beforehand, not because checking your own score is risky, but because stacking several hard searches together is. ## The short version

| Action | Search type | Affects your score? |
|---|---|---|
| Checking your own score via an app or credit reference agency | Soft | No |
| Using an eligibility checker before applying | Soft | No |
| Submitting a full credit application | Hard | Small, temporary effect |
| Several full applications in a short period | Hard (repeated) | Larger, more visible effect |
