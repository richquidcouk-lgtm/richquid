**Short answer**: Money to the Masses is the right fit if you want **investment commentary from a founder-led, named-expert brand** with the option to subscribe to **80-20 Investor** for algorithmic fund picks. RichQuid is the right fit if you want **plain-English, calculator-led explainers on the tax and regulatory changes that move UK money right now**, with everything free and the working shown on every figure. Most readers benefit from both — they cover different parts of the personal finance picture.

This is a fair head-to-head between the two sites, written by RichQuid. We&rsquo;ve tried to be honest about both — Money to the Masses is an excellent site and worth following regardless of what you think of ours.

## Where Money to the Masses wins

**1. Investment commentary with named-expert depth.** Damien Fahy spent 20 years in the City as a financial adviser before founding MTTM in 2010. When he writes about funds, ETFs, fund switching or how to read a Key Investor Information Document, the credentials are real and showing.

**2. Podcast distribution.** Two of the most popular UK money/investment podcasts. If you prefer listening to reading, MTTM is in a different league — RichQuid has no audio product.

**3. Live portfolio transparency.** Damien publishes the performance of his own £50,000 portfolio on the site. Almost nobody else in UK personal finance does this. It builds extraordinary trust.

**4. The 80-20 Investor membership.** If you want algorithmic fund-picking guidance without paying a full IFA, the paid service is genuinely useful. Bi-annual subscription works out at roughly £12/month — a fraction of what equivalent advice costs.

**5. 16 years of compounded content.** Long-tail SEO authority that a new site can&rsquo;t match overnight. Many "best fund for X" search queries will surface MTTM articles for years.

MTTM has tools but they&rsquo;re fewer, simpler and don&rsquo;t break the calculation down.

## When to use which

### Use Money to the Masses if you:

- Want investment commentary and fund analysis from a named expert
- Are happy to subscribe to a paid service for algorithmic fund picks
- Prefer audio content (podcast-led learning)
- Want to follow a transparent investor publishing his own portfolio
- Trust Damien Fahy&rsquo;s 20-year industry track record specifically

### Use RichQuid if you:

- Want clear answers to "how much tax will I pay on X" or "how does this allowance work"
- Need calculators that let you plug in your numbers and see the working
- Are navigating recent regulatory changes (April 2026 dividend rise, April 2027 cash ISA / pension IHT changes)
- Are on a UK visa, recently arrived, or planning to move abroad with UK pension or ISA
- Prefer free, open content with no paywall
- Want guides cross-linked to glossary terms and live HMRC sources

### Use both if you:

- Are saving and investing as a UK DIY investor — read MTTM for investment commentary, use RichQuid for the tax-and-allowance maths
- Want diversified sources for any financial decision (a healthy default)

## Where the two sites overlap

Both cover ISAs, pensions, mortgages, savings rates, tax and budgeting. The substantive difference is **angle**: MTTM angles toward investing and product reviews; RichQuid angles toward tax and life-event finance. On a topic like "best Stocks &amp; Shares ISA platform" you might prefer MTTM. On "how is my SIPP withdrawal taxed if I&rsquo;m living in Spain" you might prefer RichQuid.

There&rsquo;s also a tone difference. MTTM is conversational and personable — Damien&rsquo;s voice carries through. RichQuid is more measured and book-like. Neither is objectively better; preference depends on the reader.

## What we don&rsquo;t have that MTTM does

Honesty matters in comparison pages. Things RichQuid doesn&rsquo;t offer:

- **A named, credentialed founder with industry track record.** Clara Penny is a pseudonymous editorial persona; Damien Fahy is a named ex-City adviser.
- **A podcast.** No audio content yet.
- **A paid membership service.** No equivalent of 80-20 Investor.
- **Live portfolio tracking.** No internal investor publishing performance.
- **16 years of archived articles.** We&rsquo;re new.

We&rsquo;re working on closing the credentials gap (see our [methodology page](/methodology)). The other gaps reflect different choices about what RichQuid wants to be — not all of them are gaps we&rsquo;d close given the option.

## The honest one-line take

If forced to recommend one to a friend new to UK personal finance: **RichQuid for the maths, MTTM for the markets.** Most engaged readers end up using both.
