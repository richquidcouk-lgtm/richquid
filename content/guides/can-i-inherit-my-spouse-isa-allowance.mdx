Yes. When a married or civil partner ISA holder dies, the surviving spouse / civil partner gets a **one-time Additional Permitted Subscription (APS)** equal to the value of the deceased&rsquo;s ISA at the higher of date of death or date of transfer. This APS is **in addition to** the surviving spouse&rsquo;s own £20,000 annual ISA allowance and can be used over a window of typically 3 years from death (or 180 days from probate, if later). The deceased&rsquo;s ISA wrapper itself doesn&rsquo;t transfer — the survivor uses the APS to put inherited funds into their own new or existing ISAs.

This is the full mechanics for the 2025/26 tax year.

## What the APS actually is

The Additional Permitted Subscription is a special one-time ISA allowance for surviving spouses, introduced in 2015 and refined since. It works alongside the standard annual ISA limit, not replacing it.

Key features:

- **Available to**: the surviving spouse or civil partner only.
- **Amount**: equal to the value of the deceased&rsquo;s ISA at the higher of the date of death or the date of transfer.
- **Usable across**: cash ISAs, stocks &amp; shares ISAs. (LISA-specific APS rules are slightly different.)
- **Time window**: up to 3 years from the date of death, or 180 days from the date of completion of probate, whichever is later.
- **Stacks with the annual £20,000**: you keep your normal allowance AND the APS.

The APS is sometimes called the &ldquo;inherited ISA allowance&rdquo; or the &ldquo;spouse ISA allowance&rdquo;. HMRC&rsquo;s name is APS.

## Why this matters

Before the APS was introduced in 2015, when a spouse died, the ISA wrapper closed and any future income or gains on the inherited assets became taxable. For a couple with £200,000 in ISAs, this could mean significant tax going forward.

With the APS, the surviving spouse can put up to the full inherited amount back into ISA wrappers — preserving the tax-free status that the couple had built up.

For a long-married couple with substantial ISA wealth, this is one of the most valuable estate-planning provisions in UK personal finance.

## How the calculation works

The APS amount is the higher of:

- The value of the deceased&rsquo;s ISA **at the date of death**.
- The value of the deceased&rsquo;s ISA **at the date the funds are transferred to the spouse**.

Worked example:

- Sam died on 1 May 2025 with £100,000 in his ISA.
- During the 3-year continuing ISA period, the markets do well and the ISA grows to £120,000 by the date of transfer to his spouse Mira.
- Mira&rsquo;s APS = £120,000 (the higher of the two values).
- This is in addition to Mira&rsquo;s own £20,000 annual allowance for 2025/26.

The mechanism captures both the original value and any growth during the continuing ISA period, so the spouse doesn&rsquo;t lose out from market timing.

## What the spouse does with the APS

The mechanics:

1. **Notify the ISA provider** of the spouse&rsquo;s death.
2. **Wait for probate** if needed.
3. **Receive the inherited ISA funds** — either directly from the deceased&rsquo;s provider or via the executor.
4. **Open or use an existing ISA** in the spouse&rsquo;s own name. This can be at the deceased&rsquo;s provider (often the simplest) or at any other UK ISA provider.
5. **Apply the APS** when subscribing — the provider records the subscription as &ldquo;APS&rdquo; rather than standard annual subscription.
6. **Pay in up to the APS amount** within the 3-year (or 180-day-after-probate) window.

You don&rsquo;t need to use the APS in one go. You can split it across multiple ISAs and over time — as long as the total is within the APS amount and the window.

The deceased&rsquo;s ISA wrapper itself doesn&rsquo;t transfer to the spouse. The funds move into the spouse&rsquo;s estate, then back into ISA wrappers via the APS.

## Provider mechanics — &ldquo;APS subscriptions&rdquo;

Most major UK ISA providers (HSBC, NatWest, Halifax, Nationwide, AJ Bell, Hargreaves Lansdown etc.) offer specific APS processes. They&rsquo;ll:

- Open or designate an ISA to receive the APS subscription.
- Tag the subscription as &ldquo;APS&rdquo; in their HMRC reporting.
- Confirm the APS amount based on documentation from the deceased&rsquo;s provider.

If the deceased&rsquo;s ISA is at one provider and the spouse wants to use the APS at a different provider, that&rsquo;s allowed. The new provider needs documentation from the old one confirming the APS value.

## What if the deceased had multiple ISAs?

The APS is **per deceased person**, not per ISA. Add up the value of all the deceased&rsquo;s ISAs at the relevant date(s), and that&rsquo;s the APS amount available to the surviving spouse.

So if the deceased had £80,000 in a cash ISA + £150,000 in a stocks &amp; shares ISA + £30,000 in a LISA, the APS is £260,000.

The spouse can use the £260,000 APS to fund any combination of their own adult ISAs — they don&rsquo;t need to match the deceased&rsquo;s split.

## Time limits to watch

The APS time window:

- **Standard**: 3 years from the date of death.
- **Extended**: 180 days from the date probate is granted, if later than the 3-year mark.

For estates where probate is slow (complex estates, contested wills), the extension means the APS doesn&rsquo;t expire before probate is complete.

If the window expires before the APS is fully used, the unused portion is lost. The spouse can still inherit and hold the funds — they just can&rsquo;t put them back in an ISA after the deadline.

For most estates, 3 years is more than enough time. The risk is forgetting about the APS — it&rsquo;s worth a calendar reminder if you&rsquo;re an executor or beneficiary.

## What if the deceased was a non-spouse beneficiary?

The APS is **only available to a married or civil-partner spouse**. Other beneficiaries (children, friends, charities) receive the inherited funds but don&rsquo;t get any ISA allowance uplift.

So if you inherit £100,000 from a parent&rsquo;s ISA, you receive £100,000 of cash (or assets transferred out of the ISA wrapper). You can put up to your standard £20,000 annual allowance into your own ISA each year — but you don&rsquo;t get an APS for the £100,000.

## Does the APS apply to LISAs?

LISA-specific APS rules differ slightly:

- The deceased&rsquo;s LISA value adds to the overall APS amount.
- But the spouse can&rsquo;t use the APS to subscribe to a LISA (unless they themselves qualify for a LISA under the standard 18–39 opening rule).
- They can use the APS to subscribe to a cash ISA or stocks &amp; shares ISA in their own name.

For most surviving spouses, this isn&rsquo;t a constraint — the APS works for the main adult ISAs they hold.

## What about inheritance tax?

The APS is an ISA allowance mechanism — it doesn&rsquo;t affect Inheritance Tax (IHT).

For IHT purposes:

- The deceased&rsquo;s ISAs form part of their estate at death.
- Assets passing to a UK-domiciled spouse / civil partner are 100% IHT-exempt.
- So a £200,000 ISA passing to a spouse is IHT-free regardless of the rest of the estate.
- The spouse uses the APS to put the funds back into ISA wrappers.

For non-spouse beneficiaries, the deceased&rsquo;s ISAs count toward the estate and may attract 40% IHT above the nil-rate band. See our [ISA inheritance guide](/what-happens-to-my-isa-when-i-die-uk/).

## Worked example: married couple, ISA inheritance

David and Pat have been married for 25 years. David dies in May 2025 with £180,000 in ISAs. Pat is 62.

- **APS available**: £180,000 (his ISA value at death; we&rsquo;ll assume no significant change before transfer).
- **Pat&rsquo;s normal 2025/26 allowance**: £20,000.
- **Pat&rsquo;s total ISA capacity for the year**: £200,000.

Pat:

- Opens her own stocks &amp; shares ISA at the same provider (David&rsquo;s ISA was there).
- Transfers David&rsquo;s ISA assets into her new ISA via the APS process.
- Uses £180,000 of APS subscription. Plus £20,000 of her own annual allowance.
- Result: £200,000 of her own ISA wealth, all in a tax-free wrapper.

Without the APS, she would have inherited £180,000 of post-ISA money — taxed as ordinary investments going forward. The APS preserves the tax-free status.
