Most UK banks ask for exactly two things: **one form of photo ID** and **one proof of address**. That covers the large majority of applicants — students, first-time account holders, people switching banks, and anyone else already settled in the UK. If you&rsquo;ve just arrived in the country and don&rsquo;t have UK paperwork yet, see our [dedicated guide for new arrivals](/guides/how-to-open-a-bank-account-as-a-new-arrival-to-the-uk) instead — the document rules bend a little in that specific case.

This is the full list of what counts as each, plus what to do if you&rsquo;re missing one.

## What counts as photo ID

Banks generally accept any one of:

- **UK or international passport** (in date).
- **UK photocard driving licence.**
- **Biometric Residence Permit (BRP)** or **eVisa share code**, if you&rsquo;re not a British or Irish citizen.
- **National identity card**, for some EU citizens.
- **Older UK paper driving licence**, occasionally accepted alongside a second document, though most banks now expect the photocard version.

A passport is the most universally accepted single document — if you have one, use it. Without one, a photocard driving licence is the next-easiest.

## What counts as proof of address

Banks want a document showing your name and current address, usually dated within the **last 3 months** (sometimes 6 for annual documents like a council tax bill):

- **Council tax bill** (current year&rsquo;s, even if paid annually).
- **Utility bill** — gas, electricity, water, or landline (not usually mobile-only bills, and not most pay-as-you-go providers).
- **Bank, building society or credit card statement** from a different provider.
- **HMRC letter or tax coding notice.**
- **Signed tenancy or mortgage agreement.**
- **Local council or housing association letter** confirming your tenancy.
- **A benefits or pension award letter** from the DWP.

What usually **doesn&rsquo;t** count on its own: a mobile phone bill, an email or online-only statement without an official letterhead, or a document addressed to someone else even if you live there too.

## What if you only have one of the two?

A few common workarounds:

- **Living with parents or a partner, nothing in your name.** Ask the bank whether a parent/householder confirmation letter works alongside your own ID — some accept this, particularly for a first account.
- **All-digital household, no paper bills.** A bank or building society statement from an account you already hold (even a savings account from years ago) usually counts as proof of address.
- **No UK address history at all yet.** See our [new arrivals guide](/guides/how-to-open-a-bank-account-as-a-new-arrival-to-the-uk) — several banks accept employer letters, university confirmation letters, or tenancy agreements as a substitute.

## Opening a first account as a teenager or student

Banks generally don&rsquo;t require a full adult document set for a young person&rsquo;s first account:

- **Under-16 accounts** are usually opened jointly with a parent or guardian, who provides their own ID and address proof.
- **16–18 accounts** often accept a passport plus a letter from school or college confirming address, since the applicant may not yet have a utility bill in their own name.
- **Student accounts** typically accept a university enrolment or accommodation confirmation letter as proof of address alongside a passport.

## No fixed address

If you don&rsquo;t have a stable address — between tenancies, temporary accommodation, or experiencing homelessness — most major UK banks offer a **basic bank account** with relaxed documentation requirements, sometimes accepting a letter from a hostel, support worker, or local authority instead of a conventional utility bill. This is a genuine FCA-supported access route, not a workaround — ask any high-street branch about their basic bank account specifically.

## Switching from another UK bank

If you&rsquo;re already a UK bank customer switching to a new one via the **Current Account Switch Service (CASS)**, the documentation bar is usually lower — many banks will accept your existing bank statement as both identity confirmation (alongside a passport or driving licence) and proof of address, since you&rsquo;re already a verified UK banking customer elsewhere.

## Do I need a National Insurance number to open an account?

No. A National Insurance number isn&rsquo;t required to open a personal current account — it&rsquo;s used for tax and employment, not account opening. Some banks ask for it as an optional field, but it won&rsquo;t block your application if you don&rsquo;t have one yet.
