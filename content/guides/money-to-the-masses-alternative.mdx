**Short answer**: Money to the Masses is excellent but its angle (founder-led investment commentary + paid 80-20 Investor service) won&rsquo;t fit every reader. Strong alternatives — depending on what you actually want — include **RichQuid** (tax-current explainers + calculators), **Which? Money** (subscription expert testing), **MoneyHelper** (free, impartial, government-backed), **MoneySavingExpert** (mass-market deals + Martin Lewis brand) and **MoneyWeek** (premium investment magazine). Each is better than MTTM for a specific use case.

If you&rsquo;re here because MTTM doesn&rsquo;t quite click, the choice depends on what you actually want from a money site.

## Why people look for an alternative to Money to the Masses

A few common reasons:

- **You don&rsquo;t want a paid membership.** 80-20 Investor at ~£12/month is good value but some readers prefer fully free content.
- **You don&rsquo;t need investment commentary.** If you&rsquo;re focused on tax, mortgages, salary planning or budgeting rather than fund-picking, MTTM&rsquo;s investing emphasis isn&rsquo;t what you need.
- **You want a different editorial voice.** MTTM is blog-and-podcast personable. If you prefer measured, book-like reading, you&rsquo;d look elsewhere.
- **You want impartial guidance not tied to product recommendations.** MTTM uses affiliate links (clearly disclosed) — some readers prefer truly neutral guidance.
- **You want broader life-event coverage** (visa, divorce, bereavement, expat).

Each of those points to a different alternative.

## 1. RichQuid — tax-current explainers + calculators

**Best for**: readers who want clear, calculator-led explainers on UK tax, allowances and recent regulatory changes — and who want everything free.

**Strengths**:
- 9+ interactive calculators with the maths shown ([take-home pay](/tools/take-home-pay-calculator), [stamp duty](/tools/stamp-duty-calculator), [mortgage affordability](/tools/mortgage-affordability-calculator), [self-assessment](/tools/self-assessment-tax-estimator), [CGT &amp; dividend tax](/tools/capital-gains-dividend-tax-calculator), and more)
- Dedicated explainer guides on the [April 2026 dividend rate rise](/guides/dividend-tax-rate-rise-april-2026), [April 2027 cash ISA changes](/guides/cash-isa-cap-12000-april-2027), [pensions entering IHT](/guides/pensions-inheritance-tax-april-2027), and other recent regulatory shifts
- Strong on niche topics MTTM under-covers: [visa-holder finance](/guides/can-i-get-a-mortgage-on-a-visa-in-the-uk), [expat ISA rules](/guides/what-happens-to-my-isa-if-i-move-abroad), [post-2025 non-dom regime](/guides/do-non-doms-pay-tax-on-foreign-income-uk)
- [Glossary of UK money terms](/glossary) cross-linked from every guide
- Fully free, no paywall, no membership tier

**Trade-offs**:
- Newer site without MTTM&rsquo;s 16-year content archive
- No podcast
- No paid algorithmic-fund-picking service equivalent to 80-20 Investor

**When to choose**: you want the maths shown, calculators you can plug your numbers into, and current explainers on UK tax-year changes.

## 2. Which? Money — subscription expert testing

**Best for**: readers who&rsquo;ll pay a subscription for independent expert-tested recommendations.

**Strengths**:
- 60+ years of consumer-rights reputation (the Consumers&rsquo; Association — the same brand that tests washing machines)
- Named, credentialed money experts with strong E-E-A-T signalling
- Best Buy lists that aren&rsquo;t affiliate-driven (because subscriptions fund the editorial)
- Phone helpline for members
- Strong on insurance, mortgages, savings, banking

**Trade-offs**:
- The most valuable content is paywalled
- Subscription cost (~£4–£8/month depending on tier)
- Updates can be slower than commercial sites
- Less interactive tooling than RichQuid or MTTM

**When to choose**: you&rsquo;re comparing UK financial products and want independent expert testing — and you&rsquo;re happy to pay for it.

## 3. MoneyHelper — free, impartial, government-backed

**Best for**: readers who want the most trustworthy possible guidance and don&rsquo;t need product recommendations.

**Strengths**:
- **Government-backed (Money and Pensions Service, arm&rsquo;s-length body of HM Government)**
- Completely free, no ads, no affiliate links
- **Pension Wise** — free guidance appointments for over-50s, no commercial equivalent
- Phone and face-to-face support, not just web
- Strong on life-event finance (divorce, bereavement, redundancy, having a baby)

**Trade-offs**:
- Can&rsquo;t recommend specific products (by design)
- Conservative tone, slower updates on regulatory changes
- Less actionable for "best buys" questions
- Functional but not engaging design

**When to choose**: you want the safest possible source for foundational guidance, particularly around pensions or major life events.

## 4. MoneySavingExpert — mass-market deals + Martin Lewis brand

**Best for**: readers who want consumer-advocacy journalism, deals coverage and reclaim-your-money advice.

**Strengths**:
- The biggest UK personal finance brand by audience
- Martin Lewis personally signals trust on regulatory and consumer issues
- Cheap Energy Club, Credit Club, Cheap Mortgages Club — real interactive tools
- Daily news cadence on rate changes, deals, scams
- Weekly Money Tips email (~16 million subscribers — the UK&rsquo;s most-read money newsletter)
- Long-running forums — UGC depth

**Trade-offs**:
- News-heavy layout can feel overwhelming
- Affiliate-funded model means commercial alignment in some sections
- Editorial design is dated
- Best for transactional questions, less for deep planning

**When to choose**: you want to find the best current deal, reclaim something specific (PPI, council tax, packaged accounts), or follow a high-volume daily newsletter.

## 5. MoneyWeek — premium investment magazine

**Best for**: affluent readers who want curated investment-led analysis with editorial polish.

**Strengths**:
- Magazine-quality long-form content
- Named, credentialed financial journalists
- Strong on markets, funds, gold, commodities, retirement planning
- Print magazine + digital
- Future plc backing (FTSE-listed publisher)

**Trade-offs**:
- Subscription-led (freemium model — best content behind paywall)
- Investment skew may not suit consumer-finance-focused readers
- Less depth on niche topics like visa-holder mortgages or HICBC

**When to choose**: you have meaningful investment assets, want curated commentary, and prefer magazine-quality long reads over blog content.

## What about other UK personal finance sites?

A few worth mentioning for completeness:

- **This is Money** (DMG Media — Daily Mail group): news-driven, opinionated, mass-market. Good for breaking financial news. Editorial style may polarise.
- **NimbleFins**: research-led comparison content, FCA-regulated. Strong on insurance and credit cards. Less depth on tax/pensions.
- **Moneyfacts**: live rate aggregator for savings and mortgages. Industry-standard data source — but more comparison tool than editorial site.

Each suits a narrower use case than the five primary alternatives above.

## How to combine sites

Most engaged UK personal finance readers use 2-3 sites rather than one. A common mix:

- **MoneyHelper** for foundational guidance on big life events
- **One of**: RichQuid (tax + calculators) OR MoneySavingExpert (deals) — depending on what you most need
- **One of**: Which? Money (independent reviews) OR MoneyWeek (investment) — if you have meaningful assets

That gives you free foundational guidance, transactional support, and depth on at least one specialism.

## The honest one-line summary

If you found MTTM&rsquo;s paid membership or investment focus didn&rsquo;t fit your needs, **RichQuid (free + tax-led)** or **MoneyHelper (free + impartial)** are the two most direct alternatives depending on whether you want depth or neutrality. The other three on this list suit specific use cases beyond what MTTM offers.
